# MURL project write-up

My project, MURL, investigated whether a small open-weight language model could be fine-tuned to assist with grading worked Calc I–II integration solutions. Instead of judging only the final answer, the model received the integration problem, a reference solution, and a student-style sequence of steps. It was trained to return a structured JSON record containing the overall correctness label, the first incorrect step, an error category, concise evidence, student-facing feedback, and a confidence score. I built a 250-example authored benchmark spanning substitution, integration by parts, trigonometric integrals and substitutions, partial fractions, definite integrals, improper integrals, and mixed-review problems. Problem families were kept disjoint across the 155-example training, 48-example validation, and 47-example test splits.

I used QLoRA, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method, to adapt small instruction-tuned models on a 16 GB Google Colab Tesla T4. The work began with Qwen2.5-3B prompt baselines and several QLoRA recipes that tested label guidance, taxonomy guidance, balanced sampling, hierarchical multi-task weighting, and two-stage diagnosis training. For the final v0.8 comparison, I applied the same frozen recipe family to Qwen3-4B and Phi-4-mini-instruct, while retaining the historical Qwen2.5 results as anchors. The selection protocol required strong structured-output validity, correctness, error-taxonomy classification, and first-error localization rather than choosing a model on only one metric.

The selected model was Qwen3-4B with the v0.6 hierarchical multi-task recipe. On the 48-example validation split it produced 47 valid outputs, classified 35 of 48 examples correctly, achieved 0.6984 taxonomy macro-F1, and localized the first error exactly in 19 of 28 eligible cases. After the model, prompt, adapter, chat template, decoding settings, and evaluator were frozen, I ran one authorized held-out evaluation with no retries or repairs. The model produced 47/47 valid outputs, 39/47 correct labels, 0.8445 correctness macro-F1, 0.7037 taxonomy macro-F1, and exact first-error localization on 24/26 eligible examples. A later qualitative review found that 43 of 47 feedback messages scored at least 7/8 for correctness, specificity, actionability, and tone, while four mathematically material feedback failures reinforced the need for instructor oversight.

If I continued the project, I would begin a separately versioned study with a new untouched test set rather than tuning on the completed v0.8 evaluation. The highest priorities would be adding real, de-identified student solutions; obtaining faculty adjudication for annotations and feedback ratings; expanding rare error categories—especially substitution errors; and measuring calibration and inter-rater agreement. After establishing performance on authentic text transcripts, the project could extend to OCR and handwritten mathematics, controlled transcription noise, and a human-in-the-loop dashboard where instructors can inspect the evidence, correct labels, and retain final grading authority.
